In PPO, what does the clipping term ($\epsilon$, typically 0.2) on the probability
ratio $\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{old}}$ actually do?

<Rationale title="Why">
**It caps the policy change per update for stability.** Clipping the ratio to
$[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$ removes the incentive to move any action's probability
too far from the old policy in one step, which is what keeps PPO stable. It is
*not* reward clipping (a different mechanism), *not* gradient-norm clipping, and
*not* sequence truncation — those are the three tempting confusions. The
"proximal" in PPO *is* this constraint.
</Rationale>
