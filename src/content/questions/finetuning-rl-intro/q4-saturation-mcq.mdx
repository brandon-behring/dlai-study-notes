On the data-saturation curves, RL rollouts need far more examples than SFT to reach
comparable performance. The best explanation is that…

<Rationale title="Why">
A rollout's learning signal is a single **scalar reward**, far sparser than an SFT
example's full target sequence, so RL must see many more examples to accumulate comparable
signal — exactly why the rollout curve saturates last. Sequence length and optimizer
learning rates change compute cost and step size, not the signal-per-example gap that
drives the data requirement. Early reward-model noise is a real stability concern, but it
affects training reliability rather than how much data rollouts fundamentally need.
</Rationale>
