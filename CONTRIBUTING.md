# Contributing

This is a personal study-notes corpus, not an open-source project. The
contribution model is intentionally narrow:

## ✓ Welcomed

- **Issues** for content corrections, typos, broken citations, factual
  errors, unclear sections, or suggestions for clarification. Use the
  issue templates in `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/`.
- **Discussions** for:
  - Q&A about the content
  - General conversation among readers also taking the courses
  - Show-and-tell of related study aids you've made
  - Suggesting future courses to add to the corpus
- **Wiki** contributions for reader-contributed study aids (cheat sheets,
  comparison tables, etc.) that complement but don't replace the core
  chapter content.
- **Takedown requests** from DeepLearning.AI, course instructors, or
  anyone else with a substantive concern. Email
  [brandon.m.behring@gmail.com](mailto:brandon.m.behring@gmail.com) —
  48-hour response.

## ✗ Not accepted

- **Pull requests** are disabled. This corpus uses a scaffold-as-platform
  architecture: substantive technical changes (new components, styling,
  layout, build tooling) belong upstream at
  [`book-scaffold-astro`](https://github.com/brandon-behring/book-scaffold-astro).
  Content changes go through the Issues channel for triage and incorporation.

- **Content additions** that fall outside the DLAI compliance posture:
  - Quiz answers, graded-assignment solutions, lab solutions
  - Verbatim transcripts or slide reproductions
  - Courses outside the Gold-tier filter

## Why this model

Single-author editorial voice across the corpus is a feature. The polish
pass on each book is where the corpus's quality comes from — propagating
that through external PRs (even well-intentioned ones) dilutes the voice
and creates a review burden that doesn't scale.

The community-engagement surfaces (Issues, Discussions, Wiki) exist for
readers who want to give feedback, ask questions, or contribute
peripheral material without changing the core content.
